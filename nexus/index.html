Working principles

Apply to every module
Source first

Preserve the testimony

Separate a source's exact statement from interpretation, inference, and later commentary.

Traceable

Every claim has a trail

Connect claims to sources, passages, pages, dates, authors, and counterevidence.

Rights aware

Permission before publication

Track text, translation, notes, audio, image, AI-processing, and distribution rights separately.

Human judgment

AI assists; people decide

Expose uncertainty and competing interpretations. Never auto-assign a religious identity or doctrinal conclusion.

Chronology laboratory

Timeline

Keep event chronology separate from the date a source was composed, edited, copied, translated, or printed.

Dates are working models for investigation, not established results. Egyptian reign dates and biblical intervals must retain their own source and uncertainty metadata.
c. 1876 BC
Jacob enters Egypt · conditional calculation

Derived from 430 years in Egypt and a 1446 BC Exodus. Not an Egyptian attestation.

1446 BC
Proposed Exodus · early chronology

Derived from the 480-year reading in 1 Kings 6:1 and a conventional date for Solomon's fourth year. Requires independent testing.

c. 1250 BC
Proposed Exodus · later chronology

A commonly discussed Ramesside-era proposal. Test against Pi-Ramesses and Israel's attestation by c. 1208 BC.

c. 1208 BC
Merneptah Stele

Secure Egyptian reference to a people called Israel in Canaan. This is a chronological checkpoint, not an Egyptian Exodus account.

The prototype timeline is intentionally small. Verified and disputed events will be managed in the shared record system rather than hard-coded into the interface.

Document ingestion

Document intake desk

Upload or register material in stages: preserve the original, capture provenance and rights, extract text, index it, then approve derived claims.

Prototype limitation: this public page does not send files to a server, store their contents, or sync them between devices. Selecting a file below only registers its metadata in this browser. For actual document analysis, attach files in this conversation. Do not place unlicensed Bible texts or private documents in the public GitHub repository.

Register local files

Accepted for inventory: PDF, DOCX, TXT, Markdown, HTML, spreadsheets, slides, images, and audio/video. Actual content extraction and secure storage will be implemented later.

What happens after a file is supplied?

1. Preserve

Keep the original unchanged, with its name, format, origin, version and checksum.

2. Identify rights

Record owner, permission, license scope, territory, expiration and permitted uses.

3. Extract and locate

Capture text with page, section, verse, timestamp or table coordinates.

4. Index and connect

Link claims and entities to exact passages without losing the original document.

5. Review and publish

Mark review status and publish only material cleared for the intended audience and use.

Publication governance

Text and media permissions

Permission for one compendium or one type of use does not automatically grant rights to reproduce every Bible edition, study note, map, image, audio recording, or dataset.

Publication gate: do not ingest, distribute, transform, or submit a copyrighted text to an AI workflow unless the relevant license or written permission authorizes that exact use. Display rights, storage rights, indexing rights, AI-processing rights, audio rights, and redistribution rights may differ.

Permission record checklist

Complete before publication

Edition identity

Exact title, revision, language, publisher, copyright owner, source file, territory and version date.

Allowed operations

Read, display, store, cache, index, quote, compare, annotate, translate, narrate, transform, AI-process and redistribute.

Audience and distribution

Private research, public website, educational use, app, commercial use, downloads, print, audio or API.

Proof and renewal

Written permission, signed license, terms URL and date checked, notices, fees, term and renewal conditions.
