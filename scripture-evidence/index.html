Research loop

  5. Record doctrinal reception separately.
  6. Preserve revisions and unknowns.

Governing distinctions

Textual evidence differs from historical inference, scholarly hypothesis, and Catholic doctrinal authority.

A map pin is not proof of identification. Sequence is not causation. A pattern is a lead, not proof of intention. Source silence is not proof of non-occurrence.

Core rules

  • Keep source text, transcription, translation, interpretation, and conclusion separate.
  • Keep historical confidence independent from doctrinal authority.
  • Use typed relationships; do not turn sequence into causation.
  • Preserve uncertainty and competing explanations.
  • Never silently revise an earlier conclusion or rule.
  • Automated patterns generate hypotheses, not proof.
  • Record rights and provenance for every imported source.
